Design & engineering

From design to implementation

Shared SwiftUI features, design tokens, retrieval, and persistence serve both native apps; each platform owns navigation and input. Core ML MiniLM embeddings connect saved Insights on an interactive map. AES-256-GCM protects personal stores with Keychain-managed keys. Architecture and tradeoffs.

One runtime, many interactions

Answers, definitions, and tree updates share one LiteRT-LM runtime and a cancellable task queue. Stable conversation and branch IDs let answers finish after navigation without writing into another thread. Regression suites cover late results, retrieval, and storage migration. State and task ownership.

Test the default before choosing it

On two 40-case development sets, thinking on and off each totaled 74/80 objective passes. Simulator CPU median answer times rose from 23.0/23.5s to 43.7/47.6s with thinking, supporting the off default. These are pattern-scored configuration comparisons, not phone speed or general accuracy. Evaluation and limitations.

inVectus

In development

A local-first research workspace built with React, TypeScript, Rust, and Tauri. Explore where a document collection may be missing coverage, then inspect the supporting evidence before deciding what to investigate.

The project

inVectus began when my older brother was looking for a medical-school research topic. My work on Angrove had taught me how embeddings connect concepts; I saw a way to apply that knowledge to patterns of coverage across papers. I turned the idea into a local-first workspace for exploring collections, inspecting evidence, and keeping research decisions.

Design & engineering

A responsive research workspace

Typed React screens connect search, imports, evidence, and saved decisions through a Tauri command boundary. A draggable, keyboard-operable split keeps the map beside the evidence and stacks when panes become too narrow. Navigation, map focus, search presentation, and pane sizing have regression tests.

Keep a decision, not just a bookmark

Bookmarks and review decisions have separate records, so saved evidence and notes survive rescans. DuckDB holds metadata; LanceDB holds vectors. The desktop and CLI share Rust crates for local search and resumable, cancellable indexing. Embedding identities prevent comparisons across incompatible vector spaces.

A claim should name its scope

Gap scoring uses the embedding-space graph; the 2D map is for browsing. Incomplete collections, specialist vocabulary, and embedding or clustering artifacts can suggest gaps that do not hold up. Calibration stays visible, evidence stays inspectable, and a person decides what merits investigation. These are collection-scoped candidates, not proof of novelty or scientific value.
