Angrove

A private study app for philosophy and theology.

I design and build Angrove to give a study session somewhere to continue after the conversation ends. Source-backed answers lead to definitions you can save as Insights and revisit in a map. On Mac, Boards bring conversations, writing, and images into one workspace.

Design notes

From design to implementation

I own the product direction, visual and interaction design, and SwiftUI implementation, including the connections between the interface, local AI, and saved work.

Room for a visual reference

An image shares the workspace with the writing. The board can hold more than a sequence of chat messages.

Keep the way back within reach

Boards, conversations, the library, and study topics share one sidebar. Moving between kinds of work should never feel like leaving the room.

Test the default before choosing it

Before choosing Angrove’s reasoning default, I ran an 80-case evaluation. Both settings passed 74 of 80 cases, while reasoning roughly doubled latency, so I kept it off by default. This was a development configuration test, not a general accuracy claim.

Notes want a place, not a scroll

A named board gives conversations, writing, and visual references a shared place. Ideas stay beside each other instead of disappearing into a chat history.

Design notes

One local workspace

I design and build the desktop interface and its connection to a shared Rust engine. Search and indexing run locally, with resumable jobs and cancellation.

The gap has a place

The selected gap stays marked on the collection map beside its evidence. Its position can be read in the context of the other papers, while the score and supporting items remain available for inspection.

Keep a decision, not just a bookmark

Saving a gap and reviewing it are different actions. A persistent decision log keeps the evidence, notes, and review decisions available after rescanning the collection, so an earlier judgment can be revisited.

A claim should name its scope

“A gap in this collection” and “a novel idea” mean different things. The flag describes coverage within this collection; it does not establish that an idea is globally novel. The copy has to say which claim the software can support.

Show the numbers behind a judgment

Demand, coverage, and activity stay visible beside a flagged gap. The score is something to inspect, with the supporting papers close at hand.
