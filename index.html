Design notes

A score you can inspect

Demand, coverage, and activity appear above the score breakdown. The numbers behind a flagged gap remain visible.

The gap has a place

The selected gap stays marked on the collection map beside its evidence. Its position can be read in the context of the other papers.

A collection, not a universal claim

The flag describes coverage within this collection. It does not establish that an idea is globally novel.

Observations

  1. Show the numbers behind a judgmentinVectus

    When software makes a call for you, its reasoning belongs on the same screen. A flagged gap in inVectus shows demand, coverage, and activity above the score, so the verdict can be checked rather than trusted.

  2. A claim should name its scopeinVectus

    Wording is interface. “A gap in this collection” and “a novel idea” look alike in a label and mean very different things. The copy has to say which one the software can actually support.

  3. Notes want a place, not a scrollAngrove

    A chat is a sequence; study is spatial. Giving notes a named board, with room for an image beside the writing, lets ideas sit next to each other instead of scrolling away.

  4. Test the default before choosing itAngrove

    Most people never change a default, so the default is the product. Before choosing Angrove’s, I ran it through an 80-case evaluation.

  5. Keep the way back within reachAngrove

    Boards, conversations, the library, and study topics share one sidebar. Moving between kinds of work should never feel like leaving the room.
