Features

Inquiry That Keeps Its Shape.

Follow one question from the moment you ask it to the map of everything it led to.

01 · Ask

Start With the Question You Actually Have

There is nothing to set up. Ask it the way you would ask someone who has read everything. Routine questions get a direct answer; harder ones get deeper reasoning, with a short note on the approach and never raw scratch work.

02 · Check the Source

See Where It Came From

When an answer quotes the Library, the source sits right after the sentence. Tap it and the passage opens, so you can read the words in their place.

It can still be wrong, so check anything that matters.

03 · Define

Any Term, Defined in Context

Terms worth a closer look are underlined. Tap one, or long-press any word, for a definition written for this conversation rather than a dictionary entry. Save it and it becomes an Insight.

04 · Keep

Insights Gather in One Library

Every Insight you save lands in your Insight Library. Save the same term from a different conversation and its new meaning joins the same card, so you can see how a word has worked across your thinking.

05 · Branch

Follow a Tangent Without Losing Your Place

Start a branch from a quoted idea and pursue the objection, the tangent, or the harder version of the question. The original line stays exactly where you left it.

06 · Map

Subjects Take Shape on Their Own

After each answer, Angrove may add a Node Concept: the broader subject the conversation has reached. Your Insights settle around the subjects they belong to, and the Insight Tree grows into a map of what you have pursued.

07 · Midpoint

Find the Idea Between Ideas

Select two Insights and drag the handle toward the one that should weigh more. Angrove proposes candidates, and your phone picks the one whose meaning sits closest to that point.

Try it: drag the handle, then Place.

08 · Make Node

Grow an Insight Into a Subject

When one Insight deserves room of its own, promote it to a Node Concept. Angrove generates three distinct ideas beneath it and spaces them so they do not crowd what is already there.

09 · Study

Study a Subject in Three Dimensions

Choose Study on a Node Concept and the camera swings down to eye level, with its Insights spread out around it. Drag to turn it like a turntable.

10 · Return

Come Back to What’s Unfinished

Home opens with a Question of the Day drawn from an idea you left unresolved, beside a few quiet cards: a date in history, a Node Concept that has not connected yet, a term you looked up and never saved, a line you wrote yourself.

Aquinas on Justice

What does Aquinas say about the virtue of justice?

Justice is about our relationship with others: giving each what they are due. Aquinas calls it the chief of the moral virtues, because it orders us toward the common good.

“giving each their due”

Is mercy ever unjust, then?

Not for Aquinas. Mercy doesn't take away what is owed; it gives more than is owed. So mercy goes beyond justice without working against it.
